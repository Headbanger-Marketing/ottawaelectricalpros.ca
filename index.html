Before work begins

Match the wiring to how the space will be used.

Picture a townhouse or semi-detached home whose spare bedroom is becoming a nursery, a gym or an office. The panel, meter and cable routes may sit near a shared wall, which can limit where new circuits run, whatever the room becomes.

Write down what each room will hold once its use changes, such as a desk, a treadmill or a night light. Note which walls are shared, and who must agree before anything outside is altered.

Do I need new circuits when a spare room becomes a home office?

Not always. A desk and a lamp may be served by existing circuits, but added heaters, equipment or a longer run of devices can change the picture. Ask the contractor to assess the actual demand before deciding.

Should the whole plan be priced together or one room at a time?

Describing every planned change in one request helps the contractor see combined demand and sequence the work. Ask for a written scope that lists each room separately, so you can compare quotes line by line.

Who must agree before work touches a shared wall or exterior?

It depends on the property. A neighbour, a condominium corporation, a landlord or the municipality may each have a say, and some properties have none of these. Confirm who can authorize changes before requesting quotes.

What records should I gather before describing the new room use?

Collect any drawings, earlier ESA paperwork, product sheets for new equipment and photographs of the panel door and its labels. Add a sketch showing where each item will sit. Do not remove any cover to take a photograph.

If I send the form, does that confirm a visit for my project?

No. This site is for reading, and your enquiry is only reviewed. Visits, quotes and the work itself are outside it. Any provider or Licensed Electrical Contractor you deal with gives its name and a licence you should check through ESA first, then agrees the job, price and dates with you.
