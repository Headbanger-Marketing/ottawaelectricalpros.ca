Outlets and dedicated circuits in Ottawa.

An exterior receptacle on an Ottawa home has to cope with rain, cold and shared use, so the position and the protection matter as much as the plug itself. Decide what it will power as your use of the yard changes.

An extra receptacle does not establish extra capacity

Receptacle locations and circuit design answer different questions. More convenient plug-in points may share the same existing circuit. New appliances or specialty equipment may need a different connection, a dedicated circuit or other assessed work. Give the Licensed Electrical Contractor the equipment information and intended location so the proposal follows the actual use.

Prepare the scope for your Ottawa property

List what you intend to plug in outdoors, from lights to a power tool, and photograph the wall where the receptacle is wanted. Note where the nearest interior receptacle is and what the route crosses.

Ask whether the location needs weather-rated equipment and protection, whether the receptacle would share a circuit with indoor loads and whether anyone else must agree to an opening in an exterior wall.

What should the assessment establish?

The scope for exterior receptacles on a house in Ottawa opens with a list of where each one can be placed and how it is weather-protected. Below that, an entry shows whether the planned tools and lights can share a circuit with indoor loads or need a separate one. A line on the exterior wall names who may approve an opening, since a wall beside a neighbour may belong to a shared structure. Another part describes how the cable reaches the outside and how any gap is sealed. Seasonal items, string lights for example, are named last.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Can one exterior receptacle run lights and a power tool together?

That depends on the combined demand and the circuit behind it, which a contractor must assess. List every item you expect to use at once and ask whether a separate circuit is advisable.

Is a plug-in outdoor extension cord a substitute for a new receptacle?

A cord left in place is not a permanent answer, and the doorway or window it passes through can be a hazard. Ask the contractor what a fixed, weather-rated receptacle would involve at that spot.
