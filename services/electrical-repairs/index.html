A symptom is a starting point, not a diagnosis

Loss of power, flickering, recurring protective-device trips or a damaged device can have different causes. The useful information is what happened, where and when, not a guess made from equipment age. A Licensed Electrical Contractor should assess the installation within a defined diagnostic scope and explain the identified cause before repair work is approved.

Prepare the scope for your Ottawa property

Write down when the problem was first noticed, which rooms are affected and what had changed in how those rooms were used that week. Photograph any visible damage from a safe distance without touching it.

Ask which circuits the visit will examine, whether the cause will be explained in writing before repairs begin, and which devices to stop using in the meantime.

What should the assessment establish?

After a power interruption in an Ottawa home, the contractor records what was affected and what was running when the problem appeared, then names the cause or says plainly where it could not be found within the agreed scope. The findings also name devices to avoid and, in a townhouse or semi-detached home, note whether the fault is limited to your side of the shared wall. What changed in the household around that time is recorded too. Any repair is described separately from the diagnostic visit, with its own written scope.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Should I keep resetting a breaker that tripped after a power cut?

No. A breaker that trips again straight away should be left alone, and the panel cover should stay closed. Note what was running and when it happened, then pass that record to the contractor.

Could the way a room is now used be linked to a new fault?

It can be relevant. Added equipment, extension cords or heaters may change what a circuit carries. Tell the contractor what was added and where, and let them assess whether it relates to the fault.

Useful project details

  • Observed symptom and when it began
  • Rooms or devices affected
  • Equipment operating at the time
  • Recent alterations and previous repairs
  • Owner or manager who can authorize access and repairs

Only photograph labels and locations that you can see safely. Do not remove electrical covers or approach damaged equipment to gather information.
