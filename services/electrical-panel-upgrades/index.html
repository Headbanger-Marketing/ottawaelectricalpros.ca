Panel and service upgrades in Ottawa.

A new home office or gym in an Ottawa home can add loads the existing service was not planned for. Whether that means a service change, and whether the utility must take part, is for a contractor to assess.

A panel and an electrical service are different parts of the system

The panel distributes electricity to property circuits. The service is the incoming supply and associated equipment. Replacing a panel does not automatically increase the available supply. A Licensed Electrical Contractor should assess existing condition and demand, planned loads and the utility requirements before recommending equipment. Spare breaker spaces alone do not establish capacity.

Prepare the scope for your Ottawa property

Photograph the meter position and the panel door with its labels from the outside, then list the rooms whose use is changing. Note how close both sit to a shared wall and what stands in front.

Ask who contacts the utility, what the utility requires before a service change and how the household would manage while the supply is switched off. Ask also whether the written scope names each step.

What should the assessment establish?

The existing service is set beside the demand a changed room adds, and the finding can fall three ways: the panel needs work, the incoming supply does, or both do. The assessment says which, and why. If a service change is proposed, the written scope separates what the utility must do from what the contractor handles. In a townhouse or semi-detached home in Ottawa, it also records where equipment can sit beside a shared wall and who has agreed. The ESA notification is mentioned either way.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Does a bigger panel mean the utility has to be involved?

Not necessarily. A larger panel can sit on the same incoming supply, while a change to the supply itself is where utility involvement usually begins. Ask the contractor to state which of the two your plan needs.

Can the meter or panel be moved to make room in a finished space?

Relocating either one is a design decision for a contractor and, for the supply side, the utility. Do not alter anything yourself. Describe the room you want to keep clear and ask what routes are practical.

Prepare your enquiry

Useful project details

  • Reason for the upgrade and known concerns
  • Existing major loads and proposed additions
  • Panel information visible without removing covers
  • Equipment location and access restrictions
  • Preferred timing and equipment that must remain operating

Only photograph labels and locations that you can see safely. Do not remove electrical covers or approach damaged equipment to gather information.
